# Insights page facelift (Track E) design

Status: design, not yet implemented.
Target: `site/insights/index.html`.
Companion docs to update on landing: `docs/systems/metrics.md` (§10, and the three cross-references listed in section 14).

---

## 1. Why this exists, and what it must not change

The page publishes everything the metrics archive holds. It does that accurately and it is hard to read. Every chart on it carries the same weight, so nothing tells a reader where to look, and each section is introduced by two or three paragraphs of methodology before the first number appears. The complaint is that it reads as a bombardment.

This redesign changes the ordering, the weighting and the amount of prose. It changes nothing about what is measured.

**The guarantee.** Every series that is on the charted page today is on the charted page after this change, inside one of the four groups, at small size when it is not its group's headline. Nothing stops being collected. Nothing is removed from the daily archive on the `metrics-data` branch. Nothing is removed from the machine-readable page under `/insights/data/`. Nothing is removed from `data.json`. The only thing this change removes is standing prose, and section 7 says where each paragraph of it goes.

**The design goal.** A visitor who gives the page ten seconds should come away with the conclusion that the project is real and moving. Not with a tour of everything the archive holds, and not with one hero number standing in for the rest.

## 3. The four groups

The page has four groups, in this order, always: **Reach**, **Following**, **Adoption**, **Delivery**. They answer, in order: who is finding this, who chose to follow it, who is actually running it, and is it being maintained.

That is one funnel read from the outside in. A visitor finds the repository, some of them mark it, fewer run the software, and the last question is whether the thing they ran is still being worked on. Following sits second because it is the next step of the same movement Reach measures, and Adoption and Delivery keep the order and the reasoning they already had.

Each group has exactly one full-width hero chart that carries the group's story, and a body of small cards that hold everything else in that group. Nothing in a group is behind a click, an accordion, a tab or a "show more". A reader who scrolls past a group has seen every figure it holds.

### 3.1 Reach

Question: who is finding this.

Hero: **Page views**, with unique visitors as the second, dashed line, exactly as the current Reach views card draws it. Views is the direct answer to the group's question, it is the widest funnel the archive measures, and it is the one traffic series the CI confound does not touch.

| Card | Series | Kind | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero: Page views | F4, F5 | hero plot | unchanged from today's card: solid filled count line, dashed uniques line, the weekly-sum upper-bound label on `uniques` when the bundle is downsampled |
| Repository clones | F6, F7 | compact plot | keeps its standing caveat line: GitHub counts this repository's own `actions/checkout` steps here. The line names the CI activity card and links to `#delivery` |
| Referring sites | F13 | detail card | the ranked list, then the movement chart or its stated reason, unchanged in behaviour |
| Popular paths | F14 | detail card | as above |

The order is derived rather than arbitrary. Clones is the only card in the series band, and it sits directly under the hero because it is the series the hero's own caveat is about: the clone line counts this repository's CI checkouts and the views line does not, and seeing the two together is what lets a reader tell the audience from the build pipeline. The two ranked cards follow, because a snapshot of where traffic came from answers a narrower question than a daily line of how much of it there was.

### 3.2 Following

Question: who chose to follow it.

Hero: **Stars**. Of the three series in this group it is the one that actually moves: forks and watchers change a handful of times a year on this repository, while the star count is the number a reader goes to a repository page to look for. It is also the widest of the three, so the group's one full-width chart is the one with a shape worth giving the width to.

| Card | Series | Kind | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero: Stars | F1 | hero plot | a fitted y axis, and therefore **no area fill**; see below. No release markers, they sit on the Delivery hero (section 3.4) |
| Forks | F2 | compact plot | its own y axis, no longer sharing one with stars |
| Watchers | F3 | compact plot | gains a plot it does not have today; see section 3.6 |

**This group exists because Reach was answering two questions.** Page views, clones, referring sites and popular paths are traffic: people finding the project. Stars, forks and watchers are people who clicked a button to keep track of it, which is a different act with a different meaning, and they were only ever in Reach because the dissolved Growth section had nowhere else to go. Splitting them out is also what fixes the weighting: Reach would otherwise have held seven of the page's twelve cards, and it is the first group a reader meets.

**The Following hero is the one hero on the page with no area fill.** A fill reads as area measured from zero, and a cumulative counter has to be drawn on a fitted axis or the only thing the chart is for is flattened: a star count moving 60 to 64 over a month is a flat line at the top of an axis that is 94 percent empty. The two rules cannot both be satisfied by one card, so the fill is what gives way, and the card says its axis is fitted. Section 9 states what this costs: three of the four things that carry the hero hierarchy at one column still apply here, and the fourth does not.

### 3.3 Adoption

Question: who is actually running it.

Hero: **App downloads**. It is the closest thing the archive measures to somebody actually running the software, it is a cumulative counter so its chart has a real trajectory, and it is present today whatever the telemetry fleet does.

| Card | Series | Kind | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero: App downloads | F9 | hero plot | installers and archives only. Rows the archive recorded before the download split are `null` and break the line, as they do today |
| Update checks | F10 | compact plot | the feed and blockmap fetches. The card states that it counts update polling rather than installs |
| Opt-in fleet figures | F15 to F19 | detail band | below the publication threshold, the existing state note in its three wordings. Above it, Track F's cards land here |

**The hero does not change identity when telemetry clears the threshold.** A layout whose shape depends on a data value is one more state to reason about for a gain that is a judgement call, and promoting "Instances reporting" to the hero later is a two-line change somebody can make deliberately. Section 16 flags this as a call to confirm.

### 3.4 Delivery

Question: is it being maintained.

Hero: **CI activity**, the daily workflow-run count, **with the release markers painted on it**. Workflow runs are the only Delivery series with a daily shape, and annotating them with the releases that shipped is the direct answer to the group's question. The marker lane, its labels, and its caption naming any release that fell outside the drawn span move across from the Growth section unchanged.

Moving the markers here is also the arrangement most likely to draw them at all. The lane needs a release inside the chart's span; the CI series is reconstructed from the Actions API and reaches back further than any traffic or growth series, so it covers release dates that the star history does not.

| Card | Series | Kind | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero: CI activity | F11, F12 | hero plot | zero is a measured zero here and sits on the axis rather than breaking the line, as today. Carries the release-marker lane. The card names the clones card and links to `#reach` |
| Contributors | F8 | compact plot | a step series: a row is written when the total changes, so its caption says "latest change recorded" rather than "as of", exactly as the current at-a-glance card does |
| Releases | F12 | detail card | a dated list of every release the archive holds: date, tag, name. The same rows the marker lane draws from, stated as text for the releases the lane could not place inside the chart's span |

### 3.5 What stays undrawn

`series.repo.open_issues` (F21), `series.repo.downloads_total` (F22), and the five telemetry network columns in F20 are carried by `data.json` and drawn by no card today. This change draws none of them either. They remain in the archive, in the bundle, and in the static tables under `/insights/data/`, which is where they are read.

This is stated so the inventory is closed: they are not casualties of the redesign, they were never on the charted page.

### 3.6 Two series gain a plot

Watchers (F3) and Contributors (F8) are at-a-glance numbers today with no chart. Under the small-card rule in section 5.3, any card whose series carries a dated column is drawn as a compact plot. Applying that rule uniformly is cheaper than maintaining two kinds of small card, and it uses data already in the bundle with no new computation. Neither is an addition of scope in the sense the constraints forbid: no series is added, no figure is derived that the page does not already print.

## 4. The four lead figures

Four figures, one per group, shown at the top of the page as a single row, and repeated as the headline of their own group. They are the page's table of contents: each is a link to its group's anchor.

Every group has one. A group whose hero carried no number above it would be the only one, and the row would stop being a table of contents and become a selection of three groups out of four, which is a judgement the page has no grounds to make.

They are rendered by **one function with two presentations** (`summary` for the top row, `heading` for the group header), so the two printings of a figure cannot disagree about its value, its trend or its refusal reason.

No curation. Each figure is printed at whatever value it currently holds, however small. There is no rule that suppresses one because it is unflattering, and no abbreviation: `72` is `72` and `12,480` is `12,480`.

### 4.1 The figures

| Group | Lead figure | Source | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reach | **Page views** | sum of every non-null `series.views.count` | flow total since this series began |
| Following | **Stars** | newest non-null `series.stars.total` | point-in-time counter |
| Adoption | **App downloads** | newest non-null `series.repo.downloads_app` | point-in-time counter |
| Delivery | **Releases shipped** | number of entries in `releases[]` | count of dated events |

Reach's figure is the same total the current at-a-glance Views card prints, computed by the same `flowCard` path. Following's is the same reading the current at-a-glance Stars card prints, and Adoption's the same total the current App downloads card prints, both by the same `pointCard` path. Delivery's is the only one new to the page, and it is a count of rows in an array the page already carries.

Three of the four are therefore at-a-glance cards promoted rather than figures invented, which is also the arithmetic that closes section 5.3's mapping: of the eight cards in the dissolving grid, three become lead figures and the other five become compact card heads.

### 4.2 The sub-line under each figure

One line, in the existing `.stat-sub` idiom, stating the provenance of the number above it.

| Figure | Sub-line | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Page views | `since <first measured day> · <N> measured days` plus ` · <M> unmeasured` when `M > 0` | `since 2026-08-18 · 19 measured days` |
| Stars | `as of <newest measured day>` | `as of 2026-09-05` |
| App downloads | `as of <newest measured day>` | `as of 2026-09-05` |
| Releases shipped | `latest <tag> on <date>` | `latest v1.1.0 on 2026-09-04` |

On a downsampled bundle, "measured days" reads "measured weekly buckets", from the existing `rowsLabel` helper. Nothing else changes.

### 4.3 The trend indicator

One chip beside each figure, in the existing `.stat-delta` markup, with the existing four tones (`delta-up`, `delta-down`, `delta-flat`, `delta-none`). The chip carries its full explanation in a `title` attribute **and** in a visually hidden span, because a chip reading `—` announces nothing on its own. That is the page's existing `deltaChip` behaviour and it is kept.

The window is a fixed 30 days ending on the archive's newest measured day (`I.DELTA_DAYS`, `I.archiveEndDay`). It is deliberately not the selected range, so the four figures stay comparable to each other whatever the range control shows.

Write `E` for `I.archiveEndDay(data)` and `D` for `I.DAY_MS`. The two windows are inclusive and adjacent, never overlapping:

- **recent** = `[E - 29·D, E]`
- **prior** = `[E - 59·D, E - 30·D]`

For the point figure, whose delta compares against the state *before* the window rather than summing the days inside it, the existing one-day offset is kept: its window opens at `E - 30·D`, as `pointDelta` does today.

| Figure | How the trend is computed | Chip text |
|---|---|---|
| Page views | the sum of `views.count` over **recent**, minus the sum over **prior**. Each window must pass the existing `coverageGap` test on its own measured rows, at the bundle's own step (1 day daily, 7 days downsampled) | signed difference, e.g. `+1,204 · 30d` |
| Stars | the existing `pointDelta` unchanged, as App downloads below | signed difference, e.g. `+12 · 30d` |
| App downloads | the existing `pointDelta` unchanged: the newest measured value, minus the newest measured value at or before `E - 30·D` | signed difference, e.g. `+37 · 30d` |
| Releases shipped | the count of `releases[]` entries whose `date` falls in **recent**, minus the count in **prior** | signed difference, e.g. `+1 · 30d` |

**Every flow chip on the page prints a signed change.** `flowTrend` computes the chip for Page views as a lead figure and as Reach's group head, and it computes the chip on the Repository clones compact card head as well. The alternative, a lead figure printing a change beside a card two rows down printing a total, puts two quantities into one idiom with nothing but a `+` sign between them.

The one-window 30-day total that the at-a-glance chips print for those two series today does not disappear with the chip. It is stated in the meta line of the card that draws the series, as a `30d total` pair beside the span and the measured-step count, computed by the existing `flowDelta` and keyed with the span its rows actually stand for when the bundle is weekly. When `flowDelta` refuses the window, no pair is printed, which is exactly what the page does today: a refused total is not a figure, and the chip beside it already states that the window is not covered end to end.

`releases.csv` is reconstructed from `published_at`, a permanent immutable timestamp, and the collector pages the release list in full on every run, so the array holds every release the repository has ever published however young the archive is. It needs no coverage test of the kind the daily series need, and no reach test either. A release either happened on a date or it did not, so both window counts are measured whenever the array is non-empty, and zero releases in a window is a measured zero. The one thing the array cannot speak about is being empty, which section 4.5 handles.

On a downsampled bundle the flow comparison inherits the same problem `flowDelta` already documents: a whole-week bucket cannot be split at the edge of a 30-day window. The rule is the one already in the page. If the buckets that fell inside a window stand for exactly 30 days, the chip is labelled `30d`; otherwise it is labelled with the span the buckets actually stand for, and its explanation says the bundle is weekly and why the span differs. Both windows are labelled from their own spans, and if the two spans differ the comparison is refused rather than made across unequal widths.

### 4.4 When a trend cannot be stated

The chip reads `—` in the `delta-none` tone, and its explanation names what is missing. It never falls back on a number computed from a different window and labelled as this one.

| Figure | Refusal case | Explanation printed |
|---|---|---|
| Page views | the 30 days to the archive end are not covered end to end | the existing `coverageGap` sentence, naming how many of the 30 days carry no measurement |
| Page views | the older 30 days are not covered end to end | "The 30 days before this window are not measured end to end, so there is nothing to compare this total with." |
| Page views | the series has never been measured | "This metric has never been measured, so there is nothing to total." |
| Stars, App downloads | never measured, or the newest measurement predates the window's close, or the window opens before the series starts, or the nearest measurement at the window's opening is more than one step away | the four existing `pointDelta` sentences, unchanged |
| Releases shipped | the archive holds no dated measurement, so there is no newest measured day for the window to end on | "The archive holds no dated measurement to anchor a window to." |

A refused trend never suppresses the figure itself. The lead figure is printed unconditionally whenever it has a measurement behind it.

### 4.5 When a figure itself has no measurement

The value renders as the page's `DASH` glyph in `.stat-value.is-unmeasured`, and the sub-line reads `No measurement recorded.` The trend chip reads `—` with the matching reason. The group still renders its hero and its cards; a missing lead figure is not a reason to withhold a group.

For Releases shipped there is one extra rule. An empty `releases[]` is not a measured zero: the bundle produces the same empty array whether the archive holds no releases or holds no release file at all. So an empty array prints `—` with the sub-line `The archive holds no release record.`, never `0`. This is the page's null-versus-zero rule applied to the one figure derived from an array length rather than from a nullable column.

### 4.6 Formatting

Every figure and every chip goes through `I.formatCount`: thousands separated by commas, negatives with U+2212, anything that is not a finite number rendered as `DASH`. No locale formatting, no `1.2k` abbreviation, no rounding. Dates are `YYYY-MM-DD` in UTC through `I.formatDay`, as everywhere else on the page.

## 5. Layout

### 5.1 Page order

1. Nav bar (unchanged, with its links rewritten to the four groups plus Method).
2. `<h1>Insights</h1>` and one sentence.
3. The links to the plain tables and to `data.json`, and the `<noscript>` line.
4. The `BUILD:SUMMARY` region.
5. `#status-region`: the staleness banner and the unavailable/empty notice.
6. **The lead figure row**: four figures, each linking to its group.
7. The sticky range control and its resolution line, then the shared-cursor hint below it.
8. `#reach`, `#following`, `#adoption`, `#delivery`.
9. `#method`.
10. Footer.

The lead figure row sits above the range control on purpose. The figures do not move with the range, and neither half of one does: a figure's value is all-time, every measured day of its series, and the 30-day delta printed beside it is a fixed window ending on the archive's newest measured day (section 4.3). Putting them below a control that governs neither would invite the reading that it does.

**Anchors.** `#reach` survives. `#at-a-glance`, `#growth`, `#referrers` and `#instances` do not. The sitemap lists three pages and no fragments, and the four disappearing ids have existed only since this page shipped, so nothing this repository publishes points at them. If an external link to one is found later, the fix is an empty `<span id="...">` alias on the group that absorbed it, not a restored section.

### 5.2 A group's structure

```
section#<group>
  p.ch-label                      the group's channel label, static markup
  h2                              Reach | Adoption | Delivery, static markup
  p.sec-copy                      exactly one sentence (section 7.2), static markup
  div.slot#<group>                everything below is rendered by the group's renderer
    div.group-head                the lead figure in its `heading` presentation
    div.hero-band                 one hero card
    div.series-band               the compact plot cards
    div.detail-band               the ranked and list cards
```

The heading, the channel label and the caption are static markup, so they are in the served HTML and are readable without JavaScript. Everything that needs a measurement is inside the slot, which is what lets the shell empty and re-render a group on a range change without touching its heading.

A band with no cards is not rendered at all, and leaves no gap.

### 5.3 The three card kinds

| Kind | Used for | Plot | Card |
|---|---|---|---|
| **hero** | one per group | `profile: "hero"`, min width 520, height 220 narrow / 280 wide, area fill under the primary line wherever the y axis starts at zero, and none where it is fitted (section 3.2) | `.chart-card`, background `--chat`, 18px padding, `<h3>` title at 1.05rem, full meta line, every conditional hint the renderer emits |

### 5.4 Band grids

| Band | Columns | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| lead figure row | `repeat(4, minmax(0, 1fr))` | 18px |
| hero band | one full-width card | n/a |
| series band | `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(300px, 1fr))`, `align-items: start` | 18px |
| detail band | `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(420px, 1fr))`, `align-items: start` | 22px |

`minmax()` does not nest: its second argument must be a track breadth, so the two bands take a flat `1fr` and keep their real pixel minimum. `auto-fill` rather than `auto-fit`, because after the split three of the four series bands hold exactly one compact card, and `auto-fit` collapses the empty tracks and stretches that single card across the whole row, where it reads as a second hero. `auto-fill` keeps the empty tracks, so a lone compact card stays one column wide and stays compact.

At the 1120px wrap with 24px page padding the series band lands on three columns and the detail band on two. `align-items: start` keeps a taller card, such as a dimension card carrying a movement chart, from stretching its neighbours.

Every plot host keeps the existing `.chart-scroll` container, so a plot that cannot fit its minimum width scrolls inside its own card and the body never scrolls sideways. At a 300px column the compact host is 268px against a 240px minimum, so a compact card does not scroll at any width this page is read at.

### 5.5 What this fixes

Today every chart is a `.chart-card` of the same size in a single-column stack, so the page offers no answer to "where do I look first". After this change each group states one number, draws one chart at twice the height of anything else in the group, and puts the rest underneath at a size that reads as supporting evidence. Nothing is hidden to achieve that.

## 7. Copy

### 7.1 Rules for every string

No em dashes in prose. No buzzword register. Plain sentences. Plain English, and no localisation: this page is not in the i18n system and is not being added to it.

The `DASH` glyph the page prints for an unmeasured value is a glyph in a data cell, not punctuation in a sentence, and it stays as it is.

Copy that moves from one place to another moves **verbatim**, except that an em dash used as punctuation is replaced by the comma, colon or full stop the sentence needs. No other wording changes, because rewriting a sentence that was written to be exactly true is how it stops being exactly true.

### 7.2 One sentence per group

Each group carries exactly one `.sec-copy` sentence, between its heading and its slot. The sentence says what the group's chart does not prove. These four strings are the specification:

> **Reach.** Views count page loads on GitHub rather than people, and a break in a line is a day nobody measured rather than a day with no traffic.

> **Following.** Stars, forks and watchers count people who clicked a button on GitHub rather than people running the software, and each is a running total that this archive can only see from the day it started recording.

> **Adoption.** Downloads count files fetched from the release page rather than installs anyone kept, and the opt-in figures below are a floor: an instance that never switched the ping on is invisible here.

> **Delivery.** Workflow runs measure this project's own automation, not the quality of what it ships.

### 7.3 Where the removed prose goes

A new final section, `#method`, headed "How these numbers are made", holds every standing paragraph that leaves a group header, plus the two page-level regions that describe the archive rather than a figure. It sits after all four groups, so a reader reaches it only after every number.

Decision 6 governs **standing** copy: paragraphs that render whatever the data says. Those are the page's answer to a state, they appear only in that state, and they are the mechanism that keeps the page from asserting something it cannot support.

Every one of them is kept, verbatim, and each is rendered inside the card whose figure it is about rather than at section level.

## 9. Responsive behaviour

The risk in a hero-plus-cards layout is that a narrow screen collapses it into a column of identical blocks, which removes the hierarchy the layout exists to create. Four things carry the hierarchy, and they survive a single column:

1. **Height.** A hero plot is 220px at narrow widths; a compact plot is 150px. A hero card is visibly taller than anything under it at every width.
2. **Fill.** A hero's primary line carries an area fill wherever its y axis starts at zero, and no compact line carries one anywhere. Three of the four heroes qualify. Following's hero is a cumulative counter on a fitted axis, so it carries no fill (section 3.2), and that group leans on the other three carriers. This is the one place the split costs something: it is a real loss and it is stated rather than glossed, and it is smaller than the loss of putting seven of twelve cards in the group a reader meets first.
3. **Surface.** A hero card sits on `--chat`; a compact card sits on `rgba(255,255,255,0.02)`, which reads as a lighter, more recessive panel against the `--base` page.
4. **The group's lead figure**, which is a large number directly above the hero at every width.

### 9.1 Breakpoints

The lead row's breakpoints are declared. The two bands have none of their own: their column counts fall out of the track minimums in section 5.4 against the content width, which is the viewport minus 48px of page padding above 460px and minus 32px below it. The thresholds below are what those minimums resolve to. They are stated rather than forced, because forcing the round numbers would be three media queries in service of a window under 100px wide.

| Width | Lead row | Series band | Detail band | Wrap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≥ 1000px | 4 columns | 3 columns above 984px, else 2 | 2 columns | 1120px max, 24px padding |
| 910px to 999px | 2 columns, values step down to the `heading` size | 2 columns | 2 columns | 24px padding |
| 666px to 909px | 2 columns, at the `heading` size | 2 columns | 1 column | 24px padding |
| 561px to 665px | 2 columns, at the `heading` size | 1 column | 1 column | 24px padding |
| 461px to 560px | 1 column | 1 column | 1 column | 24px padding |
| ≤ 460px | 1 column | 1 column | 1 column | 16px padding, as today |

Three series columns of 300px with an 18px gap need 936px of content, which is a 984px viewport; two need 618px, which is 666px. Two detail columns of 420px with a 22px gap need 862px of content, which is a 910px viewport. The 1120px wrap leaves 1072px of content at most, which is short of a fourth series column and short of a third detail column, so neither band ever exceeds the counts in the top row.

The lead row goes to two columns below 1000px rather than staying at four, because four figures across a 950px viewport leaves under 220px each and the summary figure size is 3.2rem. At two columns the row is two rows deep, so the values step down to the `heading` size to stop the row pushing the range control off the first screen.

Two existing rules are independent of that table and are unchanged: the nav's section links hide at or below 900px, leaving the brand and the GitHub button; and the `.change-row` and `.rank-head` reflows in the dimension cards fire at 560px and 460px respectively. The nav now carries five links rather than four, which is why the hide threshold matters and why it is not raised.

The lead row's track is `minmax(0, 1fr)`, so a long label cannot push a figure wider than its share. The two bands cannot use that track: with `auto-fill` and no pixel minimum they would resolve to a single column. What keeps a long referrer host or path from widening a column there is `.chart-card { min-width: 0 }` and the existing `.rank-*` truncation with its `title` attributes, both unchanged.

### 9.2 Plots at narrow widths

The hero keeps a 520px minimum plot width, so below roughly 570px of card width it scrolls horizontally inside its own `.chart-scroll` container. That is the page's existing behaviour and the reason it exists is unchanged: below that width the day axis collapses into overlapping ticks.

Compact plots have a 240px minimum and a wider tick spacing. At or below 460px the page padding is 16px a side and a compact card's own padding is 16px a side, so the plot host is the viewport minus 64px: the minimum is cleared at 304px and above, which covers every viewport a phone presents, a 320px iPhone SE included. The minimum is 240 rather than 260 for exactly that reason. The body never scrolls sideways at any width.

## 10. Accessibility

Everything below is either the page's existing behaviour, kept, or a small improvement this change makes because it is rewriting the markup anyway.

- **Headings.** `h1` for the page, `h2` per group and for `#method`, `h3` for every card title, and `h3` for each of `#method`'s five subheadings (section 7.3). Card titles are `<p class="chart-title">` today; making them `<h3>` gives a screen reader a real outline of the page for the first time. The `.chart-title` styling is unchanged; only the element changes.
- **Colour is never the only carrier.** Every plotted line has a legend entry with its label. Every ranked bar carries its name as text. Every trend chip carries its direction as a sign in the text, not only as a tone.
- **The dash speaks.** Every `—` that stands for a refused figure carries its reason in a visually hidden span as well as in a `title`, through the existing `.vh` pattern. This is kept for the lead figures and extended to the compact card heads. A meta line's `30d total` pair is not in this category: when its total is refused the pair is not printed at all, and the chip beside it carries the reason (section 4.3).
- **Keyboard.** The range control is four real `<button>` elements, as today. The four lead figures are `<a href="#reach|#following|#adoption|#delivery">`. Nav links and the footer are anchors. No element gains a click handler without being a button or a link. The existing `:focus-visible` treatment (2px `--sky` outline, 3px offset) applies to all of them.
- **Anchors.** `scroll-margin-top: 168px` on every panel, measured against the 60px nav plus the sticky control bar, unchanged.
- **Reduced motion.** The existing `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` blocks stay: `scroll-behavior: auto` on `html`, and every animation and transition clamped to 0.001s. No new motion is introduced (section 6.5).
- **Reduced transparency.** The design system asks for a solid fallback under `prefers-reduced-transparency`. The page does not implement one today. This change adds one for its two glass surfaces, the nav strip and the range pill, falling back to `--channel` with no blur. It is three lines of CSS and it is the one design-system rule the page currently misses.
- **JavaScript disabled.** Unchanged in substance: the served HTML carries the `h1`, the group headings, the four group captions, `BUILD:SUMMARY`'s measured figures, the `<noscript>` pointer to the plain tables, and the `#method` prose, all without running a line of script. The lead figures, the charts and the card heads are drawn client side and are absent, exactly as the charts are absent today. The page does not become less readable without JavaScript than it is now, and `#method` moving into the served markup makes it slightly more so.
- **Charts are not keyboard operable**, today or after this change: uPlot's cursor and drag-zoom are pointer interactions. The answer for a keyboard or screen-reader reader is the static tables under `/insights/data/`, linked at the top of the page and again in `#method`. This is the existing posture, stated so it is not mistaken for a regression introduced here.

## 11. Page shell changes

### 11.1 Slots

`SLOT_IDS` becomes:

```js
var SLOT_IDS = ["lead-figures", "reach", "following", "adoption", "delivery", "method-coverage"];
```

and the subset that receives the unavailable/empty note is `["reach", "following", "adoption", "delivery"]` (section 8.1).

Six registered sections replace the six there are today: one for the lead figure row, one per group, and one for the method coverage line. Each group renderer builds its own bands, and a band with no cards is not rendered, so Following builds two and the other three build three.

### 11.2 What the shell keeps unchanged

`validateBundle` in full, including `checkTelemetry`'s absent-is-valid rule and the two invariants it deliberately does not enforce. `evaluateFreshness` and the banner. `rangeWindow`, `rangeSlice`, `archiveStartDay`, `archiveEndDay`, `resolutionStepDays`, `resolutionSuffix`. `SERIES_NAMES`, including the rule that `telemetry.network` is not in it and must not be added. The four ranges and the `all` default. The 15-second fetch timeout and the single relative same-origin request. `parseDay`, `formatDay`, `formatCount`, `formatInstant`, `dayGap`, `DASH`, `DELTA_DAYS`.

### 11.3 Per-card error containment

Today a throw in the referrers renderer fails only the referrers slot. With one slot per group, the same throw would take out the whole Reach group, which is a regression in blast radius. So each card is built through a wrapper:

```
buildCardSafely(band, build)
  try   build()                       the card appends itself to the band
  catch replace it with the existing slot-note slot-note-error markup,
        log the real exception to the console, and continue with the next card
```

The wrapper reuses the existing failure note wording verbatim: the section could not be rendered, the rest of the page is unaffected, the underlying data is unchanged, details are in the console. A throw in the group renderer itself, outside any card, is still contained to that group's slot by the shell, exactly as today.

Teardown is unchanged in its contract: a group renderer owns every uPlot instance it built, clears its list first so a failing destroy cannot leave a half-released instance to be destroyed twice, and re-throws the first failure after releasing the rest. A group that threw part way through still owes a teardown for what it did build, which is why `entered` is set before the render call and not after.

### 11.4 Shared figure helpers

The at-a-glance IIFE holds the figure logic today: `flowCard`, `pointCard`, `stepCard`, `flowDelta`, `pointDelta`, `stepDelta`, `coverageGap`, `deltaChip`, `rowsLabel`, `recordsLabel`, `newestMeasured`, `oldestMeasured`. That section dissolves, and five consumers now need those helpers: the lead figure row, and each of the four group renderers for their compact card heads.

They move into a small shared surface, `window.INSIGHTS_FIGURES`, declared in its own IIFE after the shell and before the group sections, in the same style as `INSIGHTS_CHARTS`. It exposes:

```
figure(spec, data, wins)    -> { label, valueText, unmeasured, sub, note, delta }
renderFigure(figure, mode)  -> an element; mode is "summary" | "heading" | "card"
chip(delta)                 -> the .stat-delta element, with its title and its .vh explanation
flowDelta(spec, data, win, step)   -> the existing one-window total, exposed because a flow
                                      card's meta line prints it (section 4.3)
releaseTrend(releases, wins)       -> the release count comparison of section 4.3
```

`flowTrend(series, field, wins, step)`, the two-window comparison of section 4.3, is computed inside `figure` and is not exported: nothing outside it consumes the arithmetic directly. It is named here because section 4.3 specifies it.

Every existing refusal sentence moves with its function, verbatim. `flowTrend` and `releaseTrend` are the only new logic on the page, and both follow the same discipline as the three that exist: they name what is missing rather than falling back on a figure computed from something else.

One rule this makes enforceable and which the spec depends on: a figure is computed exactly once per render and rendered in as many places as needed. The lead figure row and a group head printing different numbers for the same series is the failure this shape exists to prevent.

## 12. Chart toolkit changes

`INSIGHTS_CHARTS` keeps its closed set of named extension points. Three things change.

### 12.1 A size profile

`mount(host, xs, defs, settings)` gains `settings.profile`, one of `"hero"` (the default, so every existing call site is unchanged) or `"compact"`.

| | hero | compact |
|---|---|---|
| minimum plot width | 520 | 240 |
| height | 220 below a 700px host, 280 at or above it | 150 |
| x tick spacing | 58px, or 96px once the span crosses a calendar year | 96px, or 120px once the span crosses a calendar year |
| legend | shown | shown |
| default stroke width | 2 | 1.5 |

The profile is threaded through `plotSize` and therefore through `trackSize`'s resize handling, so a compact plot resizes on the same path as a hero one. Nothing else about `mount` changes: `spanGaps: false`, the UTC `tzDate`, the shared `SYNC_KEY`, the single `new uPlot(...)` and the rollback on a failed construction are all untouched.

The legend stays on compact cards deliberately. It is the only place a hovered value is printed, and the shared cursor's honesty depends on it: a chart whose span does not cover the hovered day shows a dash there, which is the true answer.

### 12.2 Release markers become a hero option

The marker lane, its label placement, its `padding` reservation and its "which releases fell outside the drawn span" caption move out of the growth section and into the toolkit, as a `settings.releases` option available to any hero card. Only the Delivery hero passes it. The drawing code, the three rules that keep the markers honest (the axis is drawn from the measured rows and never stretched to reach a release; a cumulative break is not a collapse; a fitted y axis says so), and the mono canvas stack all move across unchanged.

`settings.releases` sets the plot's top padding, because reserving the lane is half of what the option does. Passing an explicit `settings.padding` alongside it is an error and throws, rather than one of the two silently winning. A caller that asks for a lane and also sets the padding holds two ideas about the same number, and the failure mode of a silent winner is a lane painted into room that was never reserved, which no text observation of the page would show.

### 12.3 What does not change

`bind`, `isolate` and `expand` keep their current signatures and semantics, including the rule that each card is expanded on its own columns so its axis spans the days that card was measured on. That rule now applies within a group rather than within a section, which is the same rule: no two cards share an axis unless they share a history. `zeroBasedCounts` stays the default y policy, and the cards that need a fitted axis keep passing their own.

The cursor group stays page-wide and keyed on scale values, so hovering a day lands on that day in every chart that covers it, across groups. That is what makes the clones card and the CI hero readable against each other now that they sit in different groups, and it is what lets a reader compare stars in Following against views in Reach without the two sharing an axis.

### 12.4 The hint stops moving

`#chart-hint` sits directly under the sticky controls bar, outside it, and stays there. The toolkit still fills it and still chooses its wording from the number of plots on the page. It no longer places it.

That placement is real machinery rather than a detail. `syncHint` calls `placeHint`, which takes the hint's wrapper, finds the last-mounted plot's host in document order, walks up to that host's ancestor that is a direct child of `<main>`, and inserts the wrapper after it. It existed because a hint sitting under a section that drew nothing would read as a caption for something that is not there. Under the sticky bar the hint is page chrome rather than a caption, so it has one fixed place and needs no bookkeeping, and with its wrapper now a sibling in `<main>` the old code would find the last group on the page and move the hint to the bottom on every render.

So `placeHint`, `lastHost`, `childOf`, `forgetHost` and `liveHosts` are removed, together with the `liveHosts.push(host)` call in `mount` and its rollback. This is the third of the three changes section 12 opens with.

## 13. The static data page

The split between the two pages gets sharper, not weaker. The charted page is for a human with a browser. `/insights/data/` is the copy a crawler or a language model reads: real `<table>` rows, a schema.org `Dataset` block naming `data.json` as a distribution, and a text rendering of every figure. The charted page does not try to carry text for machines beyond `BUILD:SUMMARY` and the committed JSON-LD block, both of which are kept exactly as they are.

**The data page needs no new prose.** Every caveat that leaves a group header is already stated there, checked against `scripts/metrics/src/datapage.ts`:

| Caveat leaving the charted page's group headers | Already on the data page |
|---|---|
| Absent is not zero | the "How to read this" paragraph, and every `not measured` cell |
| Clones count this repository's own CI checkouts | the clones blurb |
| A measured zero in CI activity is not a gap, and reconstruction stops at the retention horizon | the CI activity blurb |
| Stars and forks are cumulative, quiet days carry the previous total, reconstructed history is a lower bound | the stars and forks blurbs |
| Referrers and paths are a trailing 14-day aggregate | the referrers and paths blurbs |
| Telemetry figures are opt-in and a lower bound | the pings section |

So the required change to `scripts/metrics/src/datapage.ts` is: **none**. Its table order, its headings and its wording stay as they are. That is a finding, not an omission, and it is worth stating because the brief asked whether the redesign gives the data page more to carry. It does not.

Two things that follow and must hold after the change:

- The data page keeps rendering from the same `DashboardData` the charts read, so the two encodings still cannot disagree about a figure.
- `summary.ts` and `datapage.ts` keep their own escaping (`escapeHtml` over `& < > " '`, ampersand first; `jsonLd` additionally escaping `<`). Referrer hostnames and paths are third-party strings and reach both pages verbatim. The charted page continues to put every such string into the document through `textContent` and `title` only, never as markup.

## 16. Calls made here that the owner may want to reverse

Each of these was decided inside this spec because the brainstorm did not cover it. Each is a single, self-contained change if the answer is different.

1. **CI activity moves from Reach to Delivery, and is Delivery's hero.** It is the only Delivery series with a daily shape, and "is it being maintained" is what a workflow-run count actually answers. The cost is that it no longer sits directly under the clones chart, which is where it was put to make the clone confound visible. The mitigations are the standing caveat on the clones card, a link in each direction, and the page-wide cursor, which already syncs on values rather than pixels and therefore never depended on adjacency.
2. **Release markers move from the stars and forks charts to the CI hero.** They were on growth so a jump could be read against what shipped. On CI they read as "what shipped, against the automation that shipped it", which fits Delivery's question, and the CI series reaches far enough back that the markers will actually draw, which on the current archive they do not.
3. **Adoption's hero is App downloads and stays App downloads** even after telemetry clears the threshold. A hero that changes identity with the data is a second layout to reason about; promoting "Instances reporting" later is a deliberate two-line change.
4. **Delivery's lead figure is "Releases shipped", not the hero's own series.** Every other group's lead figure is its hero's primary series. Here the hero is annotated with the releases the figure counts, so the two are the same subject, but the rule is not uniform and that is worth knowing.
5. **Watchers and Contributors gain a compact plot** they do not have today, as the uniform application of the small-card rule. No new data and no new computation, but it is two more charts than the page draws now.
6. **The at-a-glance section dissolves.** Its eight cards survive as three lead figures and five card heads (section 5.3), and its coverage line moves to `#method`. Nothing it printed is lost, but the page no longer has a single block where all eight numbers sit side by side.
7. **Following's hero is Stars, and it is the one hero on the page with no area fill.** Stars is the series in that group that moves and the number a reader goes looking for; forks and watchers change a handful of times a year. The fill is refused because the axis has to be fitted, and a fill under a fitted axis would read as area measured from a zero that is not there. The reverse call is a zero-based axis with a fill, which flattens the only movement the chart is for.
8. **The lead figure row carries four figures, one per group, and Following's is Stars.** The alternative was three figures and one group whose hero carried no number above it, which would have made the row a selection rather than a table of contents. The cost is that the row is two columns deep below 1000px (section 9.1).
9. **Every flow chip prints a signed change, and the 30-day total moves into the card's meta line.** Today the Views and Clones chips print a one-window total; after this change they print a change, computed by `flowTrend`, so a lead figure's chip and a card head's chip in the same idiom cannot mean two different quantities. The total is still printed, in the meta line of the card that draws the series (section 4.3). The reverse call is to keep `flowDelta` on the compact card heads and give the two chips visibly different affordances instead.
10. **Four anchors are dropped:** `#at-a-glance`, `#growth`, `#referrers` and `#instances`. The sitemap lists three pages and no fragments and nothing this repository publishes points at them, so nothing breaks (section 5.1). If an external link to one turns up, the fix is an empty `<span id="...">` alias on the group that absorbed it, which is a one-line change. `#growth` is not reused as Following's anchor, because Following is not the Growth section under a new name and an id that says otherwise would mislead whoever reads the markup next.
11. **Stars and forks lose their shared time axis as well as their shared y axis.** Call 2 moves the markers; this is the separate consequence of expanding each card on its own columns (section 12.3). The two can no longer be compared by horizontal position, only through the page-wide cursor, which lands on the same day in both.
12. **No group renders a window line.** The three the page draws today are dropped and `#method`'s coverage line states the selected range once (sections 2 and 7.3). The reverse call is one line per group slot, at the cost of the same sentence four times on a page whose stated problem is too much text.
