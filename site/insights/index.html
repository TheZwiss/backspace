Insights

Every number on this page comes from one archive: a scheduled job records the repository's traffic, stars, forks, contributors and releases once a day and commits them to a public data branch. The page reads a single bundle built from that archive at deploy time. Nothing is estimated, and nothing is fetched from anywhere but this site.

Prefer the numbers to the charts? The same archive is published as plain tables and as one machine-readable file, data.json. Both are built from this page's own bundle, so they cannot disagree with what is drawn here.

The headline measurements are written into this page at deploy time. If you are reading this sentence instead of them, this copy was not built by the deploy pipeline: the figures are in the plain tables and in data.json.

at-a-glance

At a glance

Stars, forks, watchers, views and clones since collection began, contributors and total release downloads — each with a 30-day delta. A delta appears only when the archive covers the window it describes; otherwise it reads as a dash, and a figure that could only be assembled from a different span is labelled with the span it actually covers.

reach

Reach

Page views and repository clones with their unique-visitor counts. A break in a line means nothing was measured at that point on the axis — a day, or a whole week once the bundle is bucketed by week — not that the figure fell to zero. GitHub reports views and clones from two separate endpoints and omits a day entirely when it recorded no traffic, so a quiet day arrives here as a break rather than as a zero, and the two charts can honestly disagree about which days exist. A break is not, on its own, evidence that the collector failed.

growth

Growth

Star and fork history with release tags marked on the time axis, so a jump can be read against what shipped. Both are cumulative point-in-time totals: a step the archive did not measure — a day, or a whole week once the bundle is bucketed by week — is a hole in the record and never a fall back to a lower count, and wherever a line is drawn that hole is a break in it rather than a dip. A star count does not return to zero, so it is the easiest gap on the page to misread as a collapse. A day on which nobody starred is not such a gap and carries a row of its own: the total that day is known, and it is the previous day's. History from before daily collection began is reconstructed from GitHub's permanent per-star and per-fork timestamps, which list only stars and forks that still exist, so a star since withdrawn is invisible there and those earlier totals are a lower bound rather than what the counter read at the time. What follows is built from the rows the archive holds and nothing else: when the history is too short to make a line, this section states the readings and its reasons instead of drawing one, and how a chart's axes are scaled is stated beside the chart itself.
