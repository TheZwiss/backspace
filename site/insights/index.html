Insights

Every number on this page comes from one archive: a scheduled job records the repository's traffic, stars, forks, contributors and releases once a day and commits them to a public data branch. The page reads a single bundle built from that archive at deploy time. Nothing is estimated, and nothing is fetched from anywhere but this site.

at-a-glance

At a glance

Stars, forks, watchers, views and clones since collection began, contributors and total release downloads — each with a 30-day delta. A delta appears only when the archive covers the window it describes; otherwise it reads as a dash, and a figure that could only be assembled from a different span is labelled with the span it actually covers.

reach

Reach

Page views and repository clones with their unique-visitor counts. A break in a line means nothing was measured at that point on the axis — a day, or a whole week once the bundle is bucketed by week — not that the figure fell to zero. GitHub reports views and clones from two separate endpoints and omits a day entirely when it recorded no traffic, so a quiet day arrives here as a break rather than as a zero, and the two charts can honestly disagree about which days exist. A break is not, on its own, evidence that the collector failed.

growth

Growth

Star and fork history with release tags marked on the time axis, so a jump can be read against what shipped. Both are cumulative point-in-time totals: a step the archive did not measure — a day, or a whole week once the bundle is bucketed by week — is a hole in the record and never a fall back to a lower count, and wherever a line is drawn that hole is a break in it rather than a dip. A star count does not return to zero, so it is the easiest gap on the page to misread as a collapse. What follows is built from the rows the archive holds and nothing else: when the history is too short to make a line, this section states the readings and its reasons instead of drawing one, and how a chart's axes are scaled is stated beside the chart itself.
